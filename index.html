Today’s setups

Morning stars, hammers, engulfings, breakouts and support bounces.

Top movers

Hammer: long lower wick (at least twice the body, 60% of the range), small upper wick. Morning star: big red candle, small-bodied candle, then a green candle closing above the middle of the first. Near breakout: close within the “near” distance below the highest high of the lookback, or above it. At support: the pattern’s low must be within the “near” distance of a support level from before the pattern (the lookback low, or a swing low from the last 60 candles). Bullish engulfing: a green candle whose body fully covers the previous red candle’s body, after a fall. Support bounce: one of the last 3 lows tested the lowest low of the lookback, and the latest candle closed green and above the previous close.

Pattern backtest

How often did each pattern actually work? Tested on every occurrence in about 3 years of daily data.

“Worked” means the price was higher after the period for bullish and neutral patterns, and lower for bearish ones. Green when it beat a random day. Uses your current Settings (at support or resistance, prior trend, “near” distance). Past results don’t guarantee future results, and costs, slippage and taxes are ignored.

IPO centre

Recent listings, how they’ve done since listing, and which are forming an IPO base.

What is an IPO base?

After listing, many new stocks spike, then pull back and move sideways for a few weeks while early investors sell. That sideways zone below the post-listing high is the IPO base. Investors such as William O’Neil watched for the first breakout above that high, ideally on strong volume. The radar flags a base when a stock listed in the last 2 years has spent at least 15 sessions 10% to 50% below its post-listing high, and is now within your “near” distance of that high or breaking above it.

Sector rotation

Which sectors are gaining strength against the market, based on their charts.

Rotation map

Right = stronger than the market. Up = getting stronger. Sectors usually move clockwise: Improving → Leading → Weakening → Lagging. The tail shows the last few weeks.

How to read it

Improving
Still weaker than the market but catching up fast. This is where the next leaders usually come from.
Leading
Stronger than the market and still gaining. The current winners.
Weakening
Still strong but losing pace. Often time to book some profits.
Lagging
Weaker than the market and still slowing. Usually best avoided for now.

Each sector is an equal-weighted average of its Nifty 500 stocks (for example “Metals & Mining” covers iron and steel makers). The market is the average of all of them. Breadth is the share of the sector’s stocks above their own 50-day average. Purely technical, not a prediction or advice.

Guide

What every signal, level and word on this radar means.

Candlestick patterns

A candle shows one period’s open, high, low and close. Green means it closed higher than it opened, red means lower. The thin lines (wicks) show how far the price travelled. These three are bullish reversal patterns: after a fall, they hint that buyers are stepping in. The radar only counts them when they form at support.

Hammer

One candle with a small body near the top and a long lower wick, at least twice the body. Sellers pushed the price down, but buyers pushed it back up before the close. Strongest when it appears at support after a fall.

Morning star

Three candles: a big red candle, then a small candle that shows indecision, then a strong green candle closing above the middle of the first one. It marks a shift from selling to buying.

Bullish engulfing (BE)

Two candles: a red candle, then a bigger green candle whose body completely covers (engulfs) the red one’s body. Buyers overpowered the sellers in a single session.

Market Paathshaala rules used here

Rules from the Market Paathshaala course (CA Rahul Ranka) that the radar now applies, summarised in our own words. They are a trading method, not a guarantee; test them on Pattern backtest.

Market condition
Nifty 500 above both its 20 and 50-day SMA: normal size. Below one: reduced size and a strict stop-loss. Below both: stay out or use about 30% size. Shown on the dashboard, screener and trade plan.
Reversal buying checklist
A visible fall first (5%+), a support line with clear swings, a bullish engulfing, hammer or morning star, higher volume on that candle, liquid Nifty 200 stocks, and preferably Nifty 500 above its 20 and 50 SMA.
RSI 40 / 65
RSI above 65 and holding: strong momentum. Below 40: weak. In between: sideways. 82 to 85: overheated, so avoid fresh buying and book part profits.
Stop-loss and target
Stop at the most recent clear swing low, one stop per trade and never moved lower. Target at the nearest swing high that hasn’t been broken yet.
20 / 50 SMA trailing
Hold while price is above the 20 SMA. A bearish close below the 20 SMA reduces half; a bearish close below the 50 SMA exits the rest.
Breakouts
Only when price is above the 20, 50 and 200 SMA. Best setups: resistance of up to 6 months with all SMAs rising. Be careful with gap-up breakouts that have no news.
Trendlines and supports
Trendlines need at least 3 touches and shouldn’t be steep. A horizontal support needs 2 or more touches; a close below it is a breakdown.
Pattern details
Double bottom/top: second low or high within about 1.5% of the first, with a 10%+ move in between. Morning/evening star: third candle closes past 50% of the first. Doji traded only as a doji star. Cup & handle: handle retraces no more than a third of the cup; best on weekly charts. Head & shoulders is rated lower accuracy.

Price levels and setups

Support and support bounce

Support is a price where falls have stopped before, so buyers tend to show up there. The radar uses the lowest low of the lookback and swing lows (a low with higher lows on both sides) from the last 60 candles. A support bounce means price recently tested support and the latest candle closed green and higher.

Resistance and breakout

Resistance is a price where rises have stalled before. The radar uses the highest high of the lookback (20 candles by default). Near breakout means the close is within 3% of it; Breakout means it closed above it.

Indicators

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Momentum on a 0 to 100 scale over 14 candles. Above 70 is often called overbought, below 30 oversold. 50 to 70 usually means healthy upward momentum.
EMA (exponential moving average)
The average closing price, weighted towards recent days. Price above its 50-day EMA suggests a medium-term uptrend; above its 200-day EMA, a long-term uptrend. The 20 EMA is drawn in Pattern view.
ATR (Average True Range)
How much a stock typically moves in one candle, over the last 14 candles. Used to place stop losses outside normal day-to-day noise.
Volume vs average
Today’s volume divided by the average of the previous 20 candles. 1.5× means 50% more shares traded than usual, which adds weight to a signal.
Market breadth
How many stocks in the list rose versus fell, and what share trade above their 50 EMA. It shows whether the whole market is strong or only a few stocks.
India VIX
The market’s “fear index”: expected volatility of the Nifty over the next 30 days. Higher VIX means bigger expected swings.

Trade plan

Short term (ST)
Holding for about 2 to 6 weeks. Worked out from the daily chart.
Medium term (MT)
Holding for about 3 to 6 months. Worked out from the weekly chart over the last 6 months.
Long term (LT)
Holding for a year or more. Worked out from the weekly chart over 2 years and the 200-day trend, with targets based on your expected yearly return.
Buy range
From the nearest support up to one ATR above it. Buying near support keeps the stop loss close, so you risk less per share. If the price is above this range, the plan suggests waiting for a dip.
Stop loss
The price where you exit to limit the loss if the setup fails. Set just below support, by 0.5, 1 or 1.5 ATR for a conservative, moderate or aggressive profile.
Sell range (targets)
Target 1 and Target 2, where you could book profits in parts. For ST and MT they are set by risk to reward; for LT by your expected return over 1 and 2 years.
Risk : reward
How much you could gain for each rupee you risk. 1 : 2 means the target is twice as far from your entry as the stop loss. Conservative uses 1 : 1.5 and 1 : 2.5, moderate 1 : 2 and 1 : 3, aggressive 1 : 2.5 and 1 : 4.
Position size
How many shares to buy so that hitting the stop loss costs only your chosen risk per trade (0.5%, 1% or 2% of capital by default), capped at 25% of capital in one stock.
Risk appetite
How much loss you can accept. Conservative: tight stops, small positions. Moderate: balanced. Aggressive: wider stops and bigger targets, with bigger swings along the way. Set it with the person icon at the top.

Data sources and freshness

Delayed (about 15 minutes)
Nifty 500 stocks, ETFs, NSE indices, US stocks, global indices, commodities, currencies and Bitcoin, from Yahoo Finance. The radar refreshes every 15 minutes on weekdays and every 3 hours at weekends. Nothing on this site is real-time.
Intraday charts (1 Day, 1 Week)
5-minute bars for the last 5 sessions, combined into 15-minute, 30-minute and 1-hour candles. 1-minute bars aren’t available because of Yahoo Finance’s free limits.
End of day
Stocks outside the Nifty 500, SGBs, government securities and bonds (NSE bhavcopy, after about 6:30 pm IST), delivery % and bulk/block deals (NSE).
Fundamentals
Yahoo Finance company data, refreshed about 40 stocks every 15 minutes, so each stock updates a few times a day. Promoter, FII and DII holding is not available from this source.
NSE feeds
FII/DII flows, corporate actions, announcements, results calendar and IPOs come from NSE’s website, which often blocks cloud servers. When that happens the section says so instead of showing old or made-up data.
Market status
Open or Closed is worked out from each exchange’s regular hours in its own time zone. Exchange holidays and special sessions are not detected.

Buy and sell triggers

Price alerts on this website
Open a stock, then under “Set an alert” tap a quick alert (buy range, stop loss, target) or type your own price. The bell icon lists your alerts. If browser notifications are on, you’re told when an alert triggers while the site is open.
Telegram alerts when the site is closed
1. In Telegram, message @BotFather, send /newbot and copy the token it gives you. 2. Send any message to your new bot, then open https://api.telegram.org/bot<token>/getUpdates and copy the number after "chat":{"id": 3. In your GitHub repo: Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → New repository secret. Add TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN and TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID. 4. Download alerts.csv from the bell icon and upload it to your repo. GitHub checks it every 15 minutes in market hours and messages you once per alert.
GTT orders at your broker
GTT (Good Till Triggered) orders wait at your broker until a price is hit, then place the order for you. Zerodha, Upstox, Angel One and others offer them; check your broker. Use “Copy GTT levels” on a trade plan: a buy GTT at the buy range, then an OCO GTT with the stop loss and target, so one cancels the other.
Why no automatic trading?
Placing orders automatically needs your broker’s API keys, which shouldn’t sit on a public website, and SEBI has specific rules for algorithmic trading by retail investors. Alerts plus GTT orders give you triggers while you keep the final decision.

ETFs, BeES and indices

ETF and BeES
An exchange-traded fund holds a basket (an index, gold, silver and so on) and trades like a share. BeES is the name Nippon India uses for its ETFs, for example NIFTYBEES tracks the Nifty 50 and GOLDBEES tracks gold prices.
LIQUIDBEES and liquid ETFs
These hold overnight money-market investments and stay at almost the same price, so chart patterns on them mean little. They are listed so your portfolio can include them.
Indices
Nifty 50, Bank Nifty, sector indices and others show where the market and each sector are heading. You can’t buy an index directly, so the trade plan shows levels without a position size.
“BE” on NSE
On this radar, BE means bullish engulfing. On NSE, BE is also a trading series (trade-for-trade), where every trade must be settled by delivery and intraday trading isn’t allowed.

Things to watch

    How the trend statuses work

    Each stock gets points from its daily chart: above its 200-day average +2 (below −2), above its 50-day average +1 (below −1), 50-day above 200-day +1 (below −1), RSI 50 to 75 +1, RSI under 35 −1, RSI over 75 −1 (overbought), bullish reversal pattern at support +1, breakout above the 20-day high +1, close below 20-day support −2, more than 30% below its 52-week high −1.

    Trend strengthening: 4 points or more and not overbought. Needs a look: −3 or lower. Stable: everything in between. These describe each chart’s trend; they are not instructions to buy or sell. Portfolio health is the value-weighted average of stock scores, reduced if one stock is over 25% of the portfolio, one sector is over 40%, or there are fewer than 5 stocks.

    These are automatic technical signals from price charts only. They don’t look at company results, valuation or your goals, so treat them as a starting point for your own research, not as financial advice.
